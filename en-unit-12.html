2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Part 1: In class. Part 2: Dreng buys a shirt at the market.
'Bok thây
Pơgê âu bơ̆n gô pơxrăm gah pơxrăm 12.
This morning we will study lesson 12.
'Bok thây
Năng hlabar, păng chih lơ̆m hlabar chih.
Read the book, and write in your notebook.
'Bok thây
Nĕ pơma dơnuh lơ̆m lăm pơxrăm.
Don't chat in class.
Part 2
Dreng
Ao 'brê âu kăp dôm yơ?
How much is this red shirt?
Bơngai tĕch
Pơ'dăm jĭt rơbâu.
Fifty thousand.
Dreng
Kăp jat! Apĭnh ih tĕch hơ-iơch 'biơ̆.
That’s very expensive! Please sell it a little cheaper.
Bơngai tĕch
Puơ̆n jĭt rơbâu ho.
Forty thousand, then.
Dreng
Êu, ĭnh chonh. Âu jên. Bơnê kơ ih!
OK, I’ll buy it. Here is the money. Thank you!

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

Asking the price: … kăp dôm yơ?

Ao 'brê âu kăp dôm yơ?
How much is this red shirt?
Ih tĕch rơmo dôm yơ?
How much is this cow?
2

Colours come after the noun

ao kok
white shirt
ao 'dum / ao 'brê
red shirt
xŏk găm
black hair
3

Classroom instructions

Instructions are just the verb, often with bĕ to make them softer.

Năng hlabar.
Read the book.
Chih bĕ!
Write, please!
Pơxrăm pơma nơ̆r Bahnar.
Learn to speak Bahnar.

🔊 Sounds

Review the catch sound (apostrophe): 'dum (red), 'long (wood, stick), 'biơ̆ (a little), 'bok (grandfather). Say each word twice, slowly and then fast.

🏡 Culture note

Village people bring vegetables, fruit, chickens, and baskets to sell at the market (kơchơ), and bargaining politely is normal. Bahnar is also taught in some primary schools in Kon Tum (present Quảng Ngãi) and Gia Lai, so children can learn to read and write their own language.
