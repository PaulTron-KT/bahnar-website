2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Pôm is not feeling well. His mother talks to him.
Mĕ
Pôm, e jĭ kiơ?
Pôm, what is hurting?
Pôm
Ĭnh jĭ kơ̆l, mĕ. Ĭnh gleh jat.
I have a headache, Mother. I'm very tired.
Mĕ
E hơ̆m hiĕn?
Do you have a cough?
Pôm
Uh ôh, ĭnh uh kơ hiĕn.
No, I don't have a cough.
Mĕ
Et pơgang jĭ kơ̆l âu, păng et 'dak.
Take this headache medicine and drink water.
Mĕ
E athei pơdei. Nĕ bô̆k pơxrăm ‘năr âu.
You must rest. Don't go to school today.
Pôm
Êu, bơnê kơ mĕ.
OK, thank you, Mother.

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

jĭ + body part = “… hurts”

Put jĭ (hurt) before the body part.

Jĭ kơ̆l.
Headache.
Jĭ klak.
Stomach ache.
Jĭ jat.
It hurts a lot.
2

athei = must, should

E athei pơdei.
You must rest.
Ih athei wih kơtă dang ei.
You must go home right now.
3

nĕ = don't

Put nĕ at the start to tell someone not to do something.

Nĕ bô̆k pơxrăm.
Don't go to school.
Nĕ pơma dơnuh lơ̆m lăm pơxrăm.
Don't chat in class.
4

uh kơ = not

Put uh kơ before the verb or adjective.

Ĭnh uh kơ hiĕn.
I don't have a cough.
Măng hei ĭnh uh kơ gơh tep.
Last night I couldn't sleep.

🔊 Sounds

Two words can look the same but mean different things. jĭ means “is” (Unit 1) and also “hurt”. măt means “eye”, “face” and “name”. Listen to the whole sentence.

🏡 Culture note

Bahnar people have long used plants from the forest as medicine, and older people often know many of them. Today villages also have health stations, and people use both kinds of care.
