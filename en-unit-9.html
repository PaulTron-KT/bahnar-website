2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Pôm and his grandfather look after the animals.
'Bok
Pra, e 'bôh kiơ?
Pra, what do you see?
Pra
Ĭnh 'bôh 'bar tŏ kơpô păng mĭnh tŏ rơmo.
I see two buffaloes and one cow.
'Bok
Kơpô tih loi kơ rơmo.
Buffaloes are bigger than cows.
Pra
'Bok, hnam bơ̆n 'dei dôm tŏ iĕr?
Grandpa, how many chickens do we have?
'Bok
'Dei mĭnh jĭt tŏ. Bơbe bơ̆n duh 'nao kon.
There are ten. And our goat has just had a kid.
Pra
Kla hơ̆m oei lơ̆m bri?
Are there tigers in the forest?
'Bok
Dang ei kla uh kơ 'dei lơ boih.
There are not many tigers now.

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

Comparing: loi kơ = more than

adjective + loi kơ + noun.

Kơpô tih loi kơ rơmo.
A buffalo is bigger than a cow.
Ti 'ma pran loi kơ ti 'ngieu.
The right hand is stronger than the left.
2

Same size: tŏ = as … as

Rơmo âu tih tŏ kơpô.
This cow is as big as a buffalo.
3

Male, female and young

tơno (male) and akăn (female) come after the animal. kon comes before it for a baby animal.

nhŭng tơno, nhŭng akăn
boar, sow
bơbe kon
kid (young goat)
kon kơpô
buffalo calf

🔊 Sounds

ĕ in iĕr (chicken) and iĕ (small) is short. ô in rôih (elephant) is a closed “o” as in “go”; ŏ in kŏ (dog) is a short open “o”.

🏡 Culture note

Animals are part of daily life: dogs guard the house and help in hunting, chickens and pigs are raised near the house, and buffaloes and cows work in the fields. Elephants (rôih) were once used for heavy work and travel in the Central Highlands.
