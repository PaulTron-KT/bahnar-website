2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Rang meets his teacher on the way to school. His friend Dreng is with him.
Rang
Hmach kơ ih, 'bok thây!
Hello, teacher!
'Bok thây
Hmach kơ e, Rang. E hơ̆m oei 'lơ̆ng akâu?
Hello, Rang. Are you well?
Rang
Êu, ĭnh oei 'lơ̆ng akâu. Bơnê kơ ih.
Yes, I am well. Thank you.
Rang
'Bok thây, âu jĭ bôl ĭnh.
Teacher, this is my friend.
'Bok thây
Măt e bu?
What is your name?
Dreng
Măt ĭnh jĭ Dreng.
My name is Dreng.
'Bok thây
Ĭnh hơ̆k jrơ̆m e ‘năr âu.
I am glad to meet you today.
Dreng
Bơnê kơ ih, 'bok thây.
Thank you, teacher.
Rang
Nĕ jô̆, nhôn athei bô̆k boih.
Sorry, we have to go now.
'Bok thây
Bô̆k mă 'lơ̆ng ho!
Go well! (Goodbye!)

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

Personal pronouns

Bahnar pronouns do not change form. The same word is used for “I” and “me”, “he” and “him”. Choose ih for adults and strangers, e for children and close friends.

ĭnhI, me
ihyou (polite)
eyou (familiar)
hăphe, she, him, her
nhônwe (not including you)
bơ̆nwe (including you)
iĕmyou (plural)
kăn hăpthey
2

Jĭ = “is”

Use jĭ to join two nouns.

Măt ĭnh jĭ Dreng.
My name is Dreng.
Rang jĭ bôl ĭnh.
Rang is my friend.
3

My, your, his

The owner comes after the thing owned. There is no separate word for “my” or “your”.

măt ĭnh
my name
bôl ih
your friend
mĕ hăp
his/her mother
4

Yes/no questions with hơ̆m

Put hơ̆m before the verb. The word dĭ can be added at the end. Answer êu (yes) or uh kơ (no).

Ih hơ̆m oei 'lơ̆ng akâu?
Are you well?
Hơ̆m gơh dĭ?
Is it OK? / Can (I)?

🔊 Sounds

The small curve over a vowel (the breve: ă, ĭ, ŏ, ơ̆, ô̆, ŭ, ư̆, ĕ, ê̆) means the vowel is short. Compare ĭnh (I) with a short i. The apostrophe in 'lơ̆ng and 'bok means the sound starts with a small catch in the throat.

🏡 Culture note

Bahnar people show respect with words for family. An older man is called 'bok (grandfather) and an older woman yă (grandmother), even if they are not relatives. A teacher is 'bok thây. When you meet someone, Hmach kơ ih (literally “greetings to you”) is polite for any adult. When someone leaves, people say Bô̆k mă 'lơ̆ng ho! (Go well!).
