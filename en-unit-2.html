2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Pôm visits Dreng’s house and looks at her family.
Pôm
Âu jĭ bu?
Who is this?
Dreng
Âu jĭ mĕ ĭnh. Noh jĭ bă ĭnh.
This is my mother. That is my father.
Pôm
E 'dei dôm 'nu 'nhŏng oh?
How many brothers and sisters do you have?
Dreng
Ĭnh 'dei mĭnh 'nu 'nhŏng drănglo păng 'bar 'nu oh drăkăn.
I have one older brother and two younger sisters.
Pôm
Yă 'bok e oei tơyơ?
Where do your grandparents live?
Dreng
Yă 'bok ĭnh oei tơ pơlei. Yă ĭnh kră boih.
My grandparents live in the village. My grandmother is old now.
Dreng
Ih 'dei 'nhŏng oh dĭ?
Do you have brothers and sisters?
Pôm
Ĭnh uh kơ 'dei 'nhŏng. Ĭnh 'dei mĭnh 'nu oh drănglo 'dĭk.
I have no older brother or sister. I only have one younger brother.

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

'Dei = have / there is

Use 'dei for “have”. Put uh kơ before it for “do not have”.

Ĭnh 'dei 'bar 'nu oh drăkăn.
I have two younger sisters.
Ĭnh uh kơ 'dei 'nhŏng.
I have no older brother or sister.
2

Counting people: number + 'nu + noun

When you count people, put 'nu between the number and the noun. Ask with dôm 'nu.

mĭnh 'nu kon
one child
Ih 'dei dôm 'nu 'de haioh?
How many children do you have?
3

Male and female

drănglo (male) and drăkăn (female) come after the family word.

oh drănglo
younger brother
oh drăkăn
younger sister
'nhŏng drăkăn or pơmei
older sister
4

âu (this) and noh (that)

âu points to something near; noh points to something further away.

Âu jĭ mĕ ĭnh.
This is my mother.
Noh jĭ bă ĭnh.
That is my father.

🔊 Sounds

'nh in 'nhŏng is like the “ny” in “canyon”, but it starts with a catch in the throat. Plain nh (as in nhôn) has no catch.

🏡 Culture note

Bahnar family words show age. You must know whether a brother or sister is older ('nhŏng) or younger (oh). Grandparents, parents and children often live close together, and the whole village (pơlei) helps when there is a wedding, a funeral or a new house to build.
