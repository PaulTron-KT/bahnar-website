2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Dreng eats dinner at Pôm's house.
Mĕ
Bĕ bơ̆n xŏng por!
Let’s eat!
Pôm
Ĭnh pơngot jat. ‘Năr âu bơ̆n xŏng kiơ?
I'm very hungry. What are we eating today?
Mĕ
Por, ka, păng 'bum 'blang.
Rice, fish, and cassava.
Mĕ
Dreng, e hơ̆m wă et 'dak?
Dreng, do you want to drink some water?
Dreng
Êu, ĭnh hal jat. Bơnê kơ ih.
Yes, I’m very thirsty. Thank you.
Dreng
Ka âu 'lơ̆ng jat!
This fish is delicious!
Mĕ
Athei xŏng mă phĭ ho!
Eat until you are full!

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

wă = want, like

Put wă before a verb.

Ĭnh wă xŏng por.
I want to eat.
Ĭnh wă wih.
I want to go home.
Hơ̆m wă dĭ?
Do you want it?
2

Describing words come after the noun

'dak tô̆
hot water
prit 'ngam
sweet banana
ka 'lơ̆ng
good fish
3

jat = very

jat comes after the adjective.

Ĭnh pơngot jat.
I'm very hungry.
'Dak âu tô̆ jat.
This water is very hot.
4

bĕ = let’s

Start with Bĕ bơ̆n … for “Let’s …”.

Bĕ bơ̆n xŏng por!
Let’s eat!
Bĕ bơ̆n bô̆k lah!
Let’s go!

🔊 Sounds

Listen for the difference between 'ba (rice plant, with a catch) and bă (father, short vowel, no catch). The apostrophe and the breve change the meaning.

🏡 Culture note

Rice is the heart of every meal: the Bahnar word por means both “cooked rice” and “meal”, and xŏng por (eat rice) simply means “eat”. Sticky rice ('ba tơyông) is cooked for special days. At festivals, rice wine is shared from a big jar (drăm) through long bamboo straws, and guests are always invited to eat.
