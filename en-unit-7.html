2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Dreng comes to Pôm’s house for the first time.
Dreng
Ĭnh hơ̆m gơh mŭt tơ hnam e dĭ?
May I come into your house?
Pôm
Gơh yơh! Apĭnh e oei tơ tăng dơ̆.
Of course! Please sit on the chair.
Dreng
Hnam iĕm 'dei dôm tŏ lăm?
How many rooms does your house have?
Pôm
'Dei pêng tŏ lăm. Mĕ bă ĭnh tep lơ̆m lăm âu.
There are three rooms. My parents sleep in this room.
Dreng
Mĕ e oei tơyơ?
Where is your mother?
Pôm
Mĕ ĭnh oei lơ̆m kôp. Hăp pai por.
My mother is in the kitchen. She is cooking.

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

oei tơ / oei lơ̆m = be at / be in

oei means “be (somewhere), live, sit, stay”.

Pôm oei tơ hnam.
Pôm is at home.
Pôm oei lơ̆m hnam.
Pôm is in the house.
2

tơyơ = where

tơyơ goes at the end of the question.

Mĕ e oei tơyơ?
Where is your mother?
Pôm bô̆k tơyơ?
Where is Pôm going?
3

'dei = there is / there are

'Dei pêng tŏ lăm.
There are three rooms.
4

Polite requests

Start with apĭnh (please). Ask for permission with … hơ̆m gơh … dĭ? The answer Gơh yơh! means “Of course!”.

Apĭnh ih pơdei tơ tăng dơ̆.
Please sit down on the chair.
Ĭnh hơ̆m gơh mŭt tơ hnam ih dĭ?
May I come into your house?

🔊 Sounds

ch in chơnang is like ch in “church” but softer. ŭnh (fire) ends with an nh sound like the “ny” in “canyon”.

🏡 Culture note

In the centre of a Bahnar village stands the rông, a communal house with a very tall, steep roof. The village meets there, guests are welcomed there, and young men once slept there. Family houses are traditionally built on stilts; cooking is often done in the kôp, a side room by the house.
