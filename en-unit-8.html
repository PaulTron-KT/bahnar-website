2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

A visitor stops Pôm on the road.
Tơmoi
Nĕ jô̆, hnam trưng oei tơyơ?
Excuse me, where is the school?
Pôm
Hnam trưng oei jĕ kơtua. Ih bô̆k kiơ̆ trong âu.
The school is near the bridge. Go along this road.
Pôm
Truh tơ kơtua, ih bô̆k gah 'ma.
When you get to the bridge, go to the right.
Tơmoi
Hơ̆m ataih dĭ?
Is it far?
Pôm
Uh kơ ataih. Pă 'biơ̆ ih gô truh.
It's not far. You'll be there in a little while.
Pôm
Ih truh dơ̆ng yơ?
Where do you come from?
Tơmoi
Ĭnh truh dơ̆ng Kon Tum. Bơnê kơ e!
I come from Kon Tum. Thank you!

Read the Bahnar line first, then check the English. Practise with a partner.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

Going: bô̆k / năm + tơ

Use tơ for “to”.

Ih năm tơyơ?
Where are you going?
Năm tơ âu.
Come here.
2

dơ̆ng = from

Ih truh dơ̆ng yơ?
Where do you come from?
Dơ̆ng âu truh Kon Kơtu…
From here to Kon Kơtu village…
3

Directions with gah (side)

gah 'ma (right), gah 'ngieu (left), gah âu (this side), gah to (that side).

Bô̆k gah 'ma trong.
Walk on the right side of the road.
4

Near and far

jĕ (near) and ataih (far) work like adjectives. Use uh kơ for “not”.

Hnam trưng oei jĕ kơtua.
The school is near the bridge.
Uh kơ ataih.
It’s not far.

🔊 Sounds

tr in trong and dr in drăkăn are clusters: say both sounds, t-r, d-r, as in English “tree” and “drum”.

🏡 Culture note

Many place names in the Central Highlands come from Bahnar. Kon is an old word for a village, as in Kon Kơtu, Kon Mơhar. The name Kon Tum is usually explained as “the village by the lake”. Villages are often built near a river (krong), with fields (mir) on the hills around them.
