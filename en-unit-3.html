2 · Pơma atŭm

Dialogue

Pôm helps his father count the cattle.
Bă
Blang, jô̆ năng dôm tŏ rơmo.
Blang, count how many cows there are.
Blang
Mĭnh, 'bar, pêng, puơ̆n, pơ'dăm, tơdrâu… 'Dei tơdrâu tŏ, bă.
One, two, three, four, five, six… There are six, Father.
Bă
Kơpô 'dei dôm tŏ?
How many buffaloes are there?
Blang
'Bar tŏ kơpô.
Two buffaloes.
Bă
Tơdrâu akŏp 'bar jĭ dôm?
Six plus two is how many?
Blang
Tơdrâu akŏp 'bar jĭ tơhngam.
Six plus two is eight.
Bă
'Lơ̆ng jat! E rơgei jat.
Very good! You are very clever.

3 · Grammar

Grammar

1

Big numbers

Tens are made with jĭt: 'bar jĭt (20), pêng jĭt (30). Add the units after: mĭnh jĭt 'bar (12), 'bar jĭt pơ'dăm (25). Hundreds use hrĕng, thousands rơbâu. See Appendix C for the full chart.

Hnam nhôn 'dei mĭnh jĭt 'bar tŏ jrăng.
Our house has twelve posts.
mĭnh hrĕng 'nu
one hundred people
2

Classifiers

Between a number and a noun you need a classifier: 'nu for people, tŏ (or pôm) for animals and things.

pêng 'nu bơngai
three people
'Bar tŏ rơmo.
Two cows.
'Bar tŏ tăng dơ̆.
Two chairs.
3

Adding

Use akŏp for “plus” and jĭ for “equals”.

'Bar akŏp 'bar jĭ puơ̆n.
Two plus two is four.

🔊 Sounds

hng in tơhngam (eight) is a breathy ng: breathe out softly as you say it. Bahnar has many breathy sounds like this: hm, hn, hl, hr, hng.

🏡 Culture note

Cattle and water buffaloes have long been a sign of wealth in Bahnar villages. A pair of cows (mĭnh kŏp rơmo) can pull a plough. In the past, a buffalo was offered at the great village festival, ‘năr pơchah gơ̆ng.
